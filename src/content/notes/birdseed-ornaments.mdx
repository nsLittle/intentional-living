  Living in Vermont means being careful about timing. Black bears are opportunistic foragers, and suet and seeds are a particular delight.

  The general rule of thumb is to wait until it’s been wintery long enough for bears to settle into hibernation — when snow cover has held for a few weeks and the woods feel properly quiet. In calendar terms, that usually lands somewhere between December 1 and March 31. But, as with most things in the natural world, it’s hard telling, not knowing.

  Once the timing feels right, making birdseed ornaments becomes one of my favorite simple winter crafts. They’re made from basic ingredients, easy enough for small hands to help with, and forgiving in all the ways good seasonal projects should be. There’s something deeply satisfying about shaping food for birds by hand, knowing it will be appreciated in small, fluttering visits all winter long.

  We hang them from branches near the house and check back often, watching familiar feathered neighbors return again and again. It’s a quiet way to participate in the season — attentive, careful, and rooted in place.
