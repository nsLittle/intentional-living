text: |
  Fall in Vermont has a way of bringing apples in faster than we can eat them. A bag from the orchard. A box from a neighbor. A bowl on the counter that keeps refilling itself.

  Fresh apples are wonderful, but there’s a short window where they’re at their best. Dehydrating is one way I stretch that window without trying to keep up with it all at once.

  It’s not a big production. Just slicing apples in the afternoon light, setting the dehydrator, and letting it run while the house goes on as usual. The smell alone feels like part of the season.

  Dried apples end up everywhere here — in oatmeal, tucked into lunches, eaten by the handful when someone wanders through the kitchen. They don’t ask much once they’re done. They just wait.

  For me, dehydrating apples is less about preservation and more about paying attention. Noticing when there’s plenty. Finding a way to carry a bit of fall forward, quietly.
