  Every few seasons, my girls and I build tiny homes for forest fairies. A shimmer of wonder hidden among the roots and moss.

  We wander the woods with our baskets, collecting what the land offers: a curled birch leaf for a roof, a pinecone door, stones for steps, a button for a window. Back home, we paint just enough color to make them feel half-real, half-dreamed.

  Days later, when sunlight filters through the branches, we’ll spot one tucked against a stump — our secret, smiling back at us.

  Some nagic is small.  Quiet. Made of twigs, laughter, and glue drying in the afternoon light.
