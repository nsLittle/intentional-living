  These cute little golden cups are called False Chanterelles. Which is funny, since they look almost nothing like true chanterelles beyond their gorgeous golden hue.

  I first noticed them scattered across damp forest litter, glowing softly against the browns and grays of early fall. Up close, they have thin, delicate caps with true gills underneath — a key difference from the blunt, forked ridges of real chanterelles. Their stems are slender, sometimes hollow, and far more fragile than their better-known lookalikes.

  Despite the misleading name, False Chanterelles aren’t especially convincing imposters once you slow down and really look. They feel lighter, flimsier, and less grounded — almost ornamental — as if they belong more to the forest floor’s decoration than its pantry.

  I like thinking of them as a reminder that resemblance doesn’t equal identity. In the woods, as in life, color alone isn’t enough. Texture, structure, and context matter far more than first impressions.
