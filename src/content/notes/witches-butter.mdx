  Witches Butter is one of those finds that feels half-real, half-invented — a bright, jelly-like bloom clinging to fallen wood as if it appeared overnight.

  Its color is impossible to ignore, glowing against damp bark and shadowed leaves. I’ve learned to pause when I see it, not to rush past in search of something “useful,” but to simply notice how strange and playful the forest can be.

  Historically, Witches Butter was sometimes used as a charm, pressed onto windowsills or doorways as a way to ward off evil or ill intent. Whether or not it holds any power, I like the idea behind it — that attention, beauty, and intention can offer a kind of quiet protection.

  Some things don’t ask to be collected or explained. They ask to be admired, remembered, and left exactly where they are.
