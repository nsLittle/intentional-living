text: |-
  My girls and I have been going to Cabot Apple Farm for years to pick our apples in the fall. They grow a wide range of varieties in beautifully maintained rows, with trees trained to grow into walls rather than round, spreading shapes. The orchard feels both orderly and generous at the same time.

  We make plenty with our apples — crumble apple pie, caramel apples, hand pies folded into pastry sheets. But we always make dehydrated apples, too.

  There’s something about freshly dehydrated apple slices, still warm, that feels especially comforting. They don’t need much. Just time, heat, and a little patience.

ingredients:
  - "Fresh apples (any variety)"
  - "Optional: lemon juice"
  - "Optional: cinnamon"

steps:
  - "Wash apples well. Core if you like. Slice evenly for consistent drying."
  - "If desired, toss slices lightly with lemon juice to prevent browning."
  - "Arrange apple slices in a single layer on dehydrator trays, avoiding overlap."
  - "Dehydrate at 135°F for 6–10 hours, until apples are leathery but still flexible."
  - "Let slices cool completely, then check for any remaining moisture."
  - "Store in airtight jars or containers in a cool, dark place."

notes: |
  Apples can be dried soft or crisp — both keep well. A light sprinkle of cinnamon before drying is nice but optional. If unsure, dry a little longer rather than less.
